# Hypotheses tested

“Supported” here means supported by this dataset and method. It does not imply a validated perception study or a universal dance rule.

| Hypothesis | Result | Evidence / remaining uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| One strong/weak pattern can represent ordinary schottische music | **Counterexamples found** | Hotel Califorgna and Iron Scottish oppose bass and bright accents; Jottish aligns them; La Solitaire has a different four-position asymmetry |
| Bass strength and sharp high-frequency attacks are interchangeable accent measures | **Rejected** | Opposing band profiles persist in the strongest cases across two models, linear/log flux, and three sampling tolerances; texture separation gives a further check |
| A strong musical attack should cause a visible foot impact | **Rejected for the existing Drumul mapping** | Żniwa’s held counts can carry the strongest bright attacks; no new foot contact is programmed there |
| Drumul stamps always coincide with louder/stronger musical counts | **Not supported** | Żniwa gives elevated mean bass attacks on stamps; Stary Olsa’s stamp bass is essentially equal to the block mean |
| All Drumul recordings should follow one accelerando | **Rejected** | Szikra 2016 remains near the mid-160s; Szikra 2023 increases gradually; Stary Olsa uses stronger tempo transitions |
| A reliable whole-song average is sufficient to choose a visual accent | **Unsafe** | F.I.N.A.L. has section-dependent changes in inferred bright-accent parity; the possibility of a model phase change is retained |
| A short excerpt can establish an arrangement-wide rule | **Not established** | Full-recording analysis exposes intros, endings, tempo switches, and section changes; unmatched previews cannot isolate arrangement from passage effects |
| Two beat detectors agreeing establishes the correct dance-count rate | **Rejected as a validation criterion** | Models can agree while choosing subdivisions; their shared training/model family is another dependency |
| A clean alternating measured pattern validates its pulse grid | **Rejected** | Known-answer synthetic data acquire strong/weak alternation under a wrong 4:3 grid; Zabili’s old grid fails the independent full-track check |
| Zabili is adequately described by the earlier ~126 BPM alternating-accent analysis | **Earlier conclusion withdrawn** | Both neural passes favour roughly 95 BPM and predominantly three-pulse groups; full-track accent measurements support that interpretation |
| Alio has one tempo regardless of version | **Rejected** | The measured compilation pulse differs from the Tandem/live previews and the cover; short-preview estimates are kept distinct from full maps |
| A fixed dotted/swing subdivision should be added to all schottisches | **No support found for that change** | The dominant bright offbeat peak lies near halfway in the 20 ordinary local examples with a slower selected pulse; this is not precise dancer microtiming |
| Current thuds consistently mark the programmed contact onset | **Rejected** | Eased support thresholds delay flashes by different amounts; default mazurka transfer delay is about 248 ms versus 9 ms for a Drumul stamp |
| Limiting Drumul thuds to explicit stamps reduces visual activity | **Supported arithmetically** | Fourteen stamp events replace 58 current contact events per dancer per cycle, a 76% reduction |
| That reduction necessarily makes the animation easier to learn | **Untested design hypothesis** | Requires an actual comparison with viewers/dancers; event counts cannot measure comprehension |
| All unidentified files can be assigned a dance from beat regularity | **Not supported** | Beat predictions also occur in controls that metadata identifies as non-dance material; musicality and dance suitability need other evidence |

The strongest design direction is to make a cue describe a **dance action** consistently. Audio analysis can help place that action in time once pulse level and phrase origin are known. It cannot, on its own, decide that a body should stamp, sink, lift, or transfer weight more strongly.
